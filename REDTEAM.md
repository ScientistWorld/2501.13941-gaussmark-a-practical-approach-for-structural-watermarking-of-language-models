The score does not reliably reward better research. Removing the watermark scores much lower, and stronger detection scores higher, but quality and generation cost are absent from the hidden scores. A real reduction in detector memory receives no meaningful reward. The reproduction **does beat its unwatermarked baseline in the direction reported by the paper**: frozen held-out TPR .777778 versus .106383, median p .006514 versus .416674, and marked/null AUC .931915 versus .5. For emitted reference metrics, the only value already at its theoretical best bound is **`unwatermarked_detection_rate_fpr_0.01 = 0`**. TPRs .777778/.577778 have upper bound 1; AUC .931915 and margin .671395 have upper bound 1; median .006514 and mean .057611 have lower bound 0; null FPR .106383 has lower bound 0. The implementation contains the paper's actual Gaussian parameter perturbation and normalized unperturbed log-likelihood gradient test, including correct causal token alignment. Mistral layer 20, `up_proj`, and variance 1e−5 match Table 2. Comparing the supplied method and settings with the local paper reveals these departures and coverage limitations:

- The gym fixes **Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3**; the paper reports base Mistral-7B. This is explicitly acknowledged in the scientist constraints.
- The reproduction uses 100 C4 realnewslike prefixes and one seed, versus the paper's first 1,000 prefixes and three seeds. A prompt is truncated, decoded, and retokenized; generation and detection do retain the same exact prompt and generated IDs.
- Generation uses sequential HuggingFace calls, with temperature 1 and top-p .9, rather than the paper's vLLM generation pipeline. The paper does not specify a top-p setting in the supplied text, so equivalence of that setting is unverified. The gym uses bf16 weights/gradients; a matching paper precision was not established.
- Generation may stop on EOS before 200 tokens; outputs shorter than 50 tokens are discarded. Only 91 marked and 94 null reference samples survive, and the hidden halves contain 45 and 47. Missing generations receive no failure penalty.
- A new predictable Gaussian key is generated from seed 42+i for every prompt, and its seed/path is recorded. This does not test a provider's persistent secret-key deployment or resistance to key recovery.
- Quality is a 25-prefix C4 CE proxy using one key, rather than the paper's larger real-text CE study and SuperGLUE/GSM-8K/AlpacaEval comparisons. It measures likelihood of prompt text, not quality of each submitted continuation. These same prefixes also supply generation prompts.
- Llama, Phi, rank-reduction reproduction, corruption/paraphrase robustness, and the other watermarking baselines are not covered by this hidden test. The audit's projected-noise variant is not claimed as a reproduction of the paper's rank-reduction settings.
- Hidden evaluation divides agent-supplied p-value arrays by position rather than regenerating responses or detecting on independently withheld examples. The nominal “TPR@FPR=.05” is actually fraction p<.05: observed reference null FPR is 10.64%, not a calibrated 5% operating point. This small sample does not establish a population-level violation, but the metric is not empirical TPR at a fixed measured FPR.

Brute force nearly solves this small submitted-output detection test without improving the watermark algorithm: 48 of 49 held-out marked examples pass p<.01. The total marked generation time, including reused first trials and newly timed retries, is 962.1 seconds versus 527.4 seconds for the cached single-pass reference, an 82% increase, plus detector work and key construction. Its reported generation/null ratio is 2.363 versus 1.295 for the cached reference. First-trial times are reused and new times exclude CPU key construction, so these are a documented mixed-run cost diagnostic, not a precise deployment latency benchmark. The 124 real extra candidate generations establish extra work regardless of timing comparability. The test is too small and permissive to distinguish this search from a better efficient watermark; this is bounded rejection sampling, not exhaustive enumeration of the enormous text space.

Several scoring-contract checks therefore fail. `scripts/evaluate_test.sh` chooses method names from writable `scoring/scores.json`, despite its comment promising directory enumeration. Only `watermark_detection_mistral` runs by default. Llama, Phi, SuperGLUE and GSM-8K, totaling .5 of configured experiment weight, never appear. Within Mistral, `c4_cross_entropy_ratio`, `c4_cross_entropy_delta`, and `generation_time_ratio` are explicitly omitted in `eval/test/evaluate_slice.py`. Missing/null-file/empty runs do not get worst values for every metric and experiment. Crashes may retain an earlier final score file. A task-failing method can outrank the reference through reported detection and null metrics, as fabrication demonstrates; genuine speed and quality failures can also outrank it. The all-positive and copied-input failures show that the existing discrimination constraints do catch some simple degenerate detectors.

Coefficient sign is not a remedy: every reported rank uses `abs(coefficient)` and `higher_is_better`, including the negative coefficients on lower-is-better FPRs. Normalization also prevents interpreting coefficients as unit multipliers. The impossible-value shortcut inflates the audit standard deviations of mean and median p to about 208,299 each, making legitimate changes in those two metrics nearly irrelevant. The exact ranks of future attempts depend on their cohort, but the fabricated, reordered, and moved-null outputs weakly improve every emitted reference metric they retain, so their advantage is not caused by a reversed sign or a favorable choice of units. All exact ties above are explicitly classified as shortcuts, except the honest reference-replay control.

The most consequential repairs for the next gym are to evaluate detector outputs on evaluator-owned texts, labels, prompt identities and fixed counts; recompute rather than trust submitted p-values; keep the null comparison fixed and apply the same detector to it; score quality and full generation/search cost; and emit complete worst-value results on failure or missing coverage. Method identifiers must bind to artifacts and every method on disk must be included. Train/test membership must be fixed before an agent's outputs exist, and final score files must be replaced on failure. FPR thresholds need a calibration protocol if the advertised target is an empirical FPR cap.
